What Is Nutri-Score and How Is It Calculated?

The FSAm-NPS algorithm, the updated calculation, and the evidence from the 521,000-person EPIC cohort.

← All articles

English · Türkçe

Bardino · 15 October 2026

Nutri-Score is a front-of-pack labelling system that France adopted officially in October 2017 through an inter-ministerial order, and that is officially recognised today in France, Belgium, Germany, the Netherlands, Luxembourg, Spain and Switzerland1. It sums up a product's overall nutritional quality in a single letter, from A (dark green) to E (dark orange). It is not a "healthy / unhealthy" stamp, though; it is a tool for comparing products within the same category.

FSAm-NPS: The Algorithm Behind the Letter

Behind the Nutri-Score letter is a numerical score called FSAm-NPS (Food Standards Agency modified Nutrient Profiling System). In the updated algorithm, the score for 100 g of product is the difference between two opposing sets of points2:

  • Negative points (N, up to 55): energy (kJ, 0-10), sugars (g, 0-15), saturated fat (g, 0-10), salt (g, 0-20).
  • Positive points (up to 17): protein (g, 0-7; for red meat no more than 2), fibre (g, 0-5), share of fruit, vegetables and legumes (%, 0-5). In the general algorithm, nuts and oils no longer count towards this last component; fats, oils, nuts and seeds are scored with a separate table.

If the negative points total 11 or more, protein is not counted (except for cheese). The final score gives the letter: 0 or less is A, 1-2 is B, 3-10 is C, 11-18 is D, and 19 or more is E2. Beverages have their own algorithm; water is the only beverage that can get an A3.

What changed in the updated algorithm?

The Nutri-Score scientific committee approved its report on foods in June 2022 and its report on beverages in February 20232, 3. France made the updated calculation official with an order dated 14 March 2025 and gave manufacturers two years to update their packaging4. The main changes:

  • Red meat: its protein points are capped at 2, which separates red meat from poultry2.
  • Sugar and salt: the point scales were extended and tightened for all foods (up to 15 negative points for sugars and 20 for salt)2. In 2023, milk and drinkable yoghurts were moved into the beverage algorithm3.
  • Fibre: the scale was reworked to separate wholegrain products from refined ones; full points now need more than 7.4 g of fibre per 100 g2.
  • Sweetened beverages: beverages containing non-sugar sweeteners get 4 extra negative points; water is still the only beverage that can get an A3.
  • Vegetable oils: the aim was for vegetable oils rich in unsaturated fat to move from C to B; most olive, rapeseed and walnut oils now get B, while most coconut oil stays at E2, 4.

Scientific Validation: BMJ, PLoS Medicine and Beyond

The nutrient profile score that Nutri-Score is based on, calculated with the earlier (pre-2022) version of the algorithm, has been linked with health outcomes in large cohorts. These links are observational and do not prove cause and effect on their own.

In the EPIC study published in the BMJ in 2020 (Deschasaux et al., 10 European countries, 521,324 participants, of whom 501,594 were analysed), participants were ranked by the FSAm-NPS score of their diet. The worst fifth showed a 7% higher risk of death from all causes than the best fifth (HR 1.07, 95% CI 1.03-1.10)5. In 2018 the same group had found a similar link with cancer, again in the EPIC cohort (471,495 people): HR 1.07 between the worst and best fifths6. In France's SU.VI.MAX cohort, each 1-point worsening of an index based on the FSA score was linked with a 14% higher risk of cardiovascular disease (HR 1.14); between the worst and best quarters the HR was 1.617.

Why it is not enough on its own

Nutri-Score does not directly measure the degree of processing (NOVA), the safety of additives (EFSA) or portion size. It helps you compare crisps with biscuits, but not biscuits with an apple.

Criticism of Nutri-Score

Some of the criticism focused on the letters given to traditional products, with olive oil the best-known example. Most olive, rapeseed and walnut oils, which got C under the old algorithm, moved to B under the updated one2, 4. Another limit comes from the design itself: because the score is calculated per 100 g or 100 ml, it does not take into account the size of the portion you actually eat.

Where does EU regulation stand?

In its Farm to Fork strategy, the European Commission had planned to propose a harmonised, mandatory front-of-pack nutrition label across the EU by the end of 2022. The Commission ran a public consultation on this from 13 December 2021 to 7 March 2022; at the European Parliament's last update on the file (December 2024), the proposal had still not been tabled8. Today Nutri-Score is used voluntarily in the seven countries listed above1; Turkey is not one of them.

A nutrient-profile indicator, not a brand rating

If a product scores "Nutri-Score A", that does not mean the manufacturer's overall quality has been approved. The score rates only the nutrient profile of that specific product per 100 g. Another product from the same brand can score E.

How Fit Dozi Uses Nutri-Score

In Fit Dozi, the Nutri-Score letter is set by AI (Google Gemini) on photo scans and for products in the app's own barcode list; for other barcodes, the letter comes from the Open Food Facts database. So the letter is not produced by a step-by-step calculation like the one below. The example shows how the updated algorithm is calculated by hand2:

Breakfast cereal (100 g):
Energy 1710 kJ → 5 points · Sugars 22 g → 6 · Saturated fat 2.1 g → 2 · Salt 0.95 g (380 mg sodium × 2.5) → 4. Negative points: 17.
Because the negative points are above 11, protein is not counted. Fibre 6 g → 3 · Fruit/vegetables 0% → 0.
